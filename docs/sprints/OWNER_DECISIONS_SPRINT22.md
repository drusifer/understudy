7. (2026-11-02) Records Jev is unsure about (below 0.7, or none of these) get a per-merchant work list: a `review.jsonl` sink, one line per merchant (payee, record count, amount, Jev's top guesses with probabilities, reason). The owner's answers come back through a separate `pinned.jsonl` source (`--with pinned=<path>`, default `.understudy/pinned.jsonl`, outside the run folders); pinned merchants skip Jev and are placed from the file. Pins are categories from the taxonomy, never free text. A sink is not also a source: runs stay write-once; promoting review lines into the pin file is the review step (console button later). No new sketch feature needed.
8. (2026-11-02) The owner's edited Monarch `Category` (Transfer, Credit Card Payment) is an input to a record's kind: those records are transfers, not purchases, refunds or income. Reason: money saved in a Schwab account and moved into Ridgewood checking is not income; the ETL read those deposits (e.g. $25,000 on 2026-09-09) as refunds of the Charles Schwab merchant. Baseline file: `data/Transactions_2026-11-02T19-14-07.csv` (Ridgewood income about $363k, expenses about $205k). Review-pile target and net-spending figure: use that file as the baseline; no percentage target set.
9. (2026-11-02) Widen the transfer rule so the card-side labels count as transfers ("Food & Dining: Credit Card Payment", "Transportation: Transfers" and the like), instead of relabeling in Monarch. This goes in Sprint 24; Sprint 23 stays closed as it is. The owner also wants to see what the money is spent on: a spending-by-category summary is a Sprint 24 story. Open for the story: which exact labels count (the group-prefixed forms of Transfer and Credit Card Payment only, or others), because "Transportation: Transfers" could also mean airport and ride transfers, which are spending.
10. (2026-11-03) The exported categories are not authoritative: "they are a mess and this tool should fix them". Supersedes decision 8 (the export's Category decides transfers) and the Sprint 24 rule that a record's spending category is the export's own Category first. The tool decides category and kind itself (links, Jev, the owner's pins); the export's labels become hints at most. To design in Sprint 25: what decides a transfer without trusting labels, where the category list Jev chooses from comes from, and how the owner's corrections (pins) override Jev.

13. (2026-11-03) An objective of the household sketch: connect each big lump-sum payment (a credit card payment) to the merchants it paid, using the Amex transactions; later the same for Venmo and Zelle payment histories, to add detail to the report. The general idea is "a lump becomes its details": a payment owns the charges it settled. Finding on the baseline (`data/amex-2026-ytd.csv`: 4,363 charges, 10 monthly payments of $14,709 to $27,976): paying oldest charges first lands within $39 to $985 of each payment after the first month (the first, $27,976, is mostly the December balance, which the export does not hold: $15,702 short); no payment matches a run of charges to the cent (statement closing dates, credits and fees are not in the export), so the link is an allocation with a stated remainder, not an exact reconstruction. Sprint 25 design.
13a. (2026-11-03) New Amex baseline: `data/activity-11-03-2026.csv` (git-ignored), 2025-12-01 to 2026-11-01, 5,015 lines, 11 payments ($262,002 paid; $271,961 of charges, $19,963 of credits). Paying the oldest charges first, ten of the 11 payments land within $33 to $335 of their amount (0.1% to 1.3%); the first (2025-12-12, $23,604) is $12,104 short because it mostly pays November's balance, which the export still does not hold. Counting credits as negative lines in the same order is worse (gaps up to $6,304), so credits are left to the refund link and the allocation uses charges only. After the last payment (2026-10-14) the charges since then are the open balance. The Monarch baseline stays `data/Transactions_2026-11-02T19-14-07.csv`.
